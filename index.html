    About Grub Gallery

    A collection of recipes gathered over the years — family recipes, cookbook favourites and things worth keeping from the internet. They live as plain notes and are published here so they can be cooked from, planned with and shared.

    Finding something to cook

    Type what you have into the search box. Terms narrow together, so chickpeas feta shows only recipes needing both. The tag buttons filter by meal and diet, and combine with whatever you have typed.

    Reading a recipe

    Click or tap any card to open the recipe over the page, so you never lose your place — or your half-built week. Ingredients tick off as you cook. On a computer, hovering a card previews its main ingredients.

    Planning a week

    Press + on a card to drop it into the next free day, or drag a card onto a particular day. The week starts on Saturday unless you change Starts. As soon as something is planned the week pins to the top of the page, so it stays in reach while you browse.

    Skipping a day

    Tap an empty day to mark it Skipped — eating out, leftovers, away. Tap again to free it. + then fills the next day that is neither planned nor skipped, so a skipped Friday stays skipped.

    Moving meals around

    Drag a planned day onto another to move it. If that day is already taken the two swap places, so nothing gets quietly overwritten. × clears a single day. Dragging needs a mouse — on a phone, use +.

    The shopping list

    Shopping list gathers every ingredient across the planned meals and groups them, so three recipes wanting Parmesan appear together with each amount. Quantities are listed rather than added up — easier to eye up at the shop than to trust a total built from “a good pinch”. Salt, pepper, oil and water are left out. Tick items off as you go, or Print the list on its own to take a paper copy.

    Printing the week

    Print week gives you the meals — seven days with their photos, for the fridge. The shopping list prints separately from its own panel, so each sheet does one job.

    Sharing a week

    The address bar always holds the current plan, so there is nothing to press: copy the URL and send it to whoever is cooking or doing the shop. Opening someone else's link simply shows their week, and editing it makes it yours.

    Come back later without a link — a bookmark, or just the address — and the last week you had is restored.
