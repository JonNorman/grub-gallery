Shepherd's Pie

Serves 5-6 · 1h20m

lambpotatocheesedinner

Lamb mince in a thick red wine gravy with peas, under buttery mash and a crisp Parmesan crust.

Before you start heat the oven to 180°C fan once the filling is made.

Method

To make the filling

  1. Heat the 1½ tbsp olive oil in a large frying pan over a medium-high heat.
  2. Cook the 2 garlic cloves and 1 onion for 1 minute.
  3. Add the 1 carrot, 1 celery stick, ¾ tsp dried thyme and ¾ tsp dried rosemary and cook for 3 minutes until soft and sweet.
  4. Turn the heat to high, add the 750g lamb mince and cook, breaking it up, until browned.
  5. Stir in the 35g plain flour.
  6. Add the 4 tbsp tomato purée, 500ml beef stock, 125ml red wine, 1 beef stock cube, 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce and 2 bay leaves.
  7. Bring to a simmer, then cook at a steady, rapid simmer over a medium heat for about 30 minutes, stirring now and then, until it is a thick gravy.
  8. Season with the ¾ tsp salt and ½ tsp black pepper, and adjust to taste.
  9. Tip into a 1.5L pie dish and stir in the 150g frozen peas.

To make the mash

  1. Heat the oven to 180°C fan.
  2. Put the 1.2kg potatoes in a large pan, cover with water by about 10cm and boil for about 15 minutes until soft.
  3. Drain, return to the pan off the heat and leave to steam dry for 30 seconds.
  4. Add the 30g unsalted butter and mash until it melts.
  5. Add the 170ml milk, season, and mash until smooth but still spreadable, adding a splash more milk if needed.

To assemble and bake

  1. Spread the hot mash over the filling and drag a fork across the top to texture it.
  2. Sprinkle with the 2-3 tbsp Parmesan and drizzle over the 30g melted butter.
  3. Bake at 180°C fan for 30-40 minutes, until deep golden and bubbling at the edges, and piping hot in the middle when checked with a knife.
  4. Rest for 5 minutes, then scatter over the thyme leaves to serve.

Notes

  • The gravy does not thicken any further in the oven, so cook it down to the thickness you want before assembling.
  • Cooling the filling first (1-2 hours in the fridge, or overnight) stops the mash sinking into it and makes it easier to spread. A short spell in the freezer while the potatoes cook does the job faster.
  • Spread the mash while it is hot; cold mash is hard to spread.
  • If you leave out the carrot and celery, add 1 tsp sugar in their place.
  • Beef mince works in place of the lamb, which makes it a cottage pie. Water works in place of the red wine.
  • For a richer topping, stir a large handful of grated cheese into the mash.
  • Gluten-free: skip the flour and thicken the gravy with 2 tbsp cornflour mixed with a splash of water, and leave out the Worcestershire sauce.
  • Make ahead: assemble without baking, cool, then fridge or freeze. Thaw completely before baking, since a frozen pie takes too long to heat through.
