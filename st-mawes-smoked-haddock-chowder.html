St Mawes Smoked Haddock Chowder

Serves 4-6 · 50m

porkfishpotatolunchdinner

A chowder more substantial than a soup, filling enough for lunch or supper with some fresh bread.

Before you start heat the oven to 180°C fan.

For the chowder

Method

To make the croutons

  1. Spread the 80g bread out on a baking tin, drizzle with half of the 2 tbsp olive oil and add a light sprinkling of sea salt.
  2. Bake at 180°C fan for 3 minutes, then toss so they brown evenly.
  3. Return to the oven for a further 3 minutes, or until golden. Set aside.

To poach the haddock

  1. Put the 400g smoked haddock and 1 bay leaf in a roasting tin and pour over the 1L whole milk.
  2. Cover with foil and bake at 180°C fan for 12 minutes.
  3. Discard the bay leaf. Peel the skin from the haddock and flake the flesh into a bowl. Set aside, reserving the milk.

To make the chowder

  1. Heat the rest of the olive oil in a large saucepan over a medium-high heat until very hot. Fry the 6 rashers smoked streaky bacon until golden and crispy.
  2. Reduce the heat, add the 50g butter and let it melt.
  3. Add the 2 onions, 3 celery sticks, ½ fennel bulb and 4 garlic cloves and sweat them in the butter until tender and translucent.
  4. Add the 50g plain flour and cook for 2 minutes, stirring.
  5. Gradually add the reserved milk, stirring to make a thick sauce. Leave to cook over a very low heat.
  6. Meanwhile, put the 400g waxy potatoes in a saucepan with the 300ml fish stock and bring to the boil. Cook for 8 minutes.
  7. Tip the potatoes and their stock into the gently simmering chowder base.
  8. Add the flaked haddock, 165g tinned sweetcorn, 3 tbsp dill and the zest and juice of the lemon, and season with salt and freshly ground black pepper.
  9. Cook for 2 minutes more to bring the flavours together.

To serve

  1. Garnish with the croutons and 1 spring onion and serve immediately.

Notes

From The Hidden Hut by Simon Stallard.
