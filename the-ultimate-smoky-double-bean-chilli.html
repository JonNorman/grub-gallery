The Ultimate Smoky Double-Bean Chilli

Serves 6-8 · 1h

vegetarianbeansone-potdinner

A meat-free chilli of black and red kidney beans slowly simmered with ancho and chipotle chillies, warm spices and dark chocolate in a rich tomato base.

Ingredients

To serve

Method

  1. Soak the 1 dried ancho chilli in hot water to soften.
  2. Heat the 3 tbsp olive oil in a large heavy-bottomed pan over a medium heat.
  3. Add the 2 large onions and 2 red peppers and cook for 8-10 minutes, until soft and translucent — give them time.
  4. Add the 4 garlic cloves and cook for another minute, until fragrant.
  5. Stir in the 2 tsp ground cumin, 2 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp ground cinnamon, ½-1 tsp chilli powder and 1 heaped tbsp chipotle chilli paste.
  6. Cook for 1-2 minutes to toast the spices, adding a splash more oil if needed.
  7. Lift the ancho chilli out of the water, roughly chop it and add it to the pan with the 2 tbsp tomato purée.
  8. Cook for 2-3 minutes, stirring frequently, until the purée deepens in colour. Add a splash of water if it starts to stick.
  9. Stir in the 400g chopped tomatoes, 250ml stock, 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce and 20g dark chocolate.
  10. Tip in the 570g jar of black beans and the 700g jar of red kidney beans, both with their bean stock, and stir well to combine.
  11. Bring to a gentle simmer, then reduce the heat to low.
  12. Cook uncovered for 40-50 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chilli has thickened and the flavours have melded.
  13. If you are serving with rice, get it on to cook while the chilli simmers.
  14. Check the chilli for seasoning once it is rich and thick.
  15. Divide the rice between bowls and spoon the chilli over the top.
  16. Top with sour cream or yoghurt, sliced avocado, pickled red onions, fresh coriander and grated cheddar or crumbled feta, then finish with a squeeze of lime.

Notes

The ancho chilli is optional, but it adds a real depth of smoky heat.

Better the next day — it only improves as it sits, so it is a good one to make ahead.

Crunchy tortilla chips are great for dunking.

Most Worcestershire sauce contains anchovies; use a vegetarian version to keep it meat-free, and swap the topping dairy for plant-based alternatives to make it vegan.
